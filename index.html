A multimodal eval instrument

ToneRater

An autorater for customer-support replies — including replies with screenshots and PDFs. It scores tone and accuracy as separate isolated judges, runs them as a multi-judge panel, and lets you watch a thin dataset game its own accuracy. Built to learn how AI evals actually work.

How to explore this — ~60 seconds, in order

An autorater is an AI that grades another AI's output. This one grades customer-support replies. The point isn't that it works — it's watching how evaluation itself fails and gets fixed. Walk these four steps:

  1. Run v1 on the realistic dataset. One judge asks "is this reply good?" Read the misses — it passes replies that are warm but factually wrong (an invented button, "refunds are always instant"). One judge can't grade two things at once.
  2. Switch to v2 and run again. Now tone and accuracy are graded by two separate judges. The warm-but-wrong replies get caught. The model didn't change — the eval design did.
  3. Flip the dataset to gamed. Accuracy jumps to ~100%. Not because the judge improved — because the test got easy. A high score on a thin dataset is how an eval lies to you.
  4. Scroll to "Be the grader." Label a few replies yourself, then see how often the AI judge agreed with you. Those human labels are exactly what "golden" data is — and closing that gap is the real job.

Every behavior here maps to a concept from Anthropic's Demystifying evals for AI agents — isolated per-dimension judges, balanced problem sets, calibrating a model grader against human labels.
